Car Detailing Invoice Checklist

TheFixer Designs guide · 2026-10-31

A car detailing invoice does more than ask for payment. It is the last thing a client sees from you, and it decides whether they remember a polished professional or a rushed transaction. A clear invoice also protects you when a client later asks what was included or questions an extra charge. Use this checklist before you send every detailing invoice so nothing important is missing.

Your business and client details

Start with the basics, and get them right every time. A client should be able to contact you and pay you without searching for anything.

  • Your business name, phone, email and website or booking link
  • The client name and the best way to reach them
  • The service address if you are a mobile detailer
  • A unique invoice number and the date of service

The vehicle, clearly identified

Detailing clients often own more than one vehicle, and fleet or dealership work can involve many. Identify the vehicle on every invoice so there is never confusion about which car was serviced.

Record the make, model, color and plate or a short description. If the vehicle arrived with existing damage such as deep scratches, chips or stained upholstery, note it in a short condition line. That single line can save a long conversation later.

Services itemized by package and add-on

List the package you performed by name, then each add-on on its own line. Interior and exterior packages mean different things to different clients, so a short description under each line helps them see the value of what they paid for.

  • The main package, for example exterior wash and decontamination or full interior detail
  • Add-ons such as engine bay cleaning, headlight restoration, pet hair removal or odor treatment
  • Paint correction or coating work, with the areas covered
  • Any extra time for heavy soiling, described plainly rather than hidden in the total

Products, travel and other charges

If you charge separately for premium products, a travel fee for distant addresses, or water and power when the client cannot provide them, show those as their own lines. Clients accept extra charges far more easily when they can see exactly what each one is for, and you avoid the impression that the price changed without warning.

Deposits, totals and how to pay

If the client paid a deposit to hold the booking, subtract it and show the balance due. Make the total impossible to miss, and state the payment methods you accept along with when payment is due, such as on completion or within a set number of days.

Add any tax required in your area. Check local rules for whether detailing services are taxed where you work.

Aftercare notes and the next booking

Finish with a short aftercare note, such as how long to wait before washing after a coating or how to care for treated leather. Then invite the next appointment. A simple line suggesting a maintenance wash on a regular schedule, with your booking link, turns a single job into a returning client.

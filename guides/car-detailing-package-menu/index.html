How to Set Up a Car Detailing Package Menu

TheFixer Designs guide · 2026-11-08

Setting up a clear menu for your car detailing business is one of the most effective ways to reduce confusion and increase bookings. When potential clients see exactly what they get, they feel more confident in their purchase. This guide helps you structure three distinct packages, define clear rules for vehicle size and condition, and present your services in a way that looks professional on a smartphone screen.

On this page

  1. Define Your Three Core Packages
  2. Establish Vehicle Size and Condition Rules
  3. List Strategic Add-Ons
  4. Optimize Your Menu for Mobile Viewing

Define Your Three Core Packages

Start by creating three tiers that cater to different needs. The first package should be a basic maintenance wash and interior vacuum. This is for regular customers who want to keep their car clean between deep cleans. The second package should be a comprehensive detail, including clay bar treatment, machine polish, and a full interior shampoo. This targets owners who want their car to look brand new. The third package should be a premium restoration service for high-mileage vehicles or those with significant wear, including paint correction and engine bay cleaning.

Each package must have a distinct purpose. Avoid overlapping services too much, as this confuses customers. The basic package saves time for busy owners. The comprehensive package offers value for those who care about aesthetics. The premium package justifies a higher effort level for neglected vehicles. By clearly separating these, you help clients self-select the service that matches their budget and expectations.

  • Basic: Exterior wash, wheel cleaning, interior vacuum, window cleaning.
  • Comprehensive: All basic services plus clay bar, one-step polish, leather conditioning, and carpet shampoo.
  • Premium: All comprehensive services plus multi-stage paint correction, engine bay detail, and headlight restoration.

Establish Vehicle Size and Condition Rules

To protect your profit margins, you must define what is included in each package. Start with vehicle size. Sedans and small SUVs fit the standard package. Larger vehicles like trucks, vans, or large SUVs require an additional charge. This extra charge should be a fixed amount or a part of the total that reflects the extra time and materials needed. State this rule clearly so there are no surprises at the driveway.

Condition is equally important. If a vehicle is excessively dirty, has pet hair, or has stains that require special treatment, these are considered add-ons. You should have a policy that allows you to adjust the price if the car is in worse condition than expected. This protects you from losing money on jobs that take twice as long as estimated. Always reserve the right to inspect the vehicle before starting work.

  • Standard size: Sedans, coupes, and compact SUVs.
  • Large size: Trucks, full-size SUVs, and vans (apply a fixed surcharge).
  • Condition check: Excessive mud, pet hair, or stains require an additional fee or are excluded from standard packages.

List Strategic Add-Ons

Add-ons are a great way to increase your average ticket value without complicating your main packages. Keep this list short and focused on high-demand services. Common add-ons include ceramic coating, paint protection film, odor removal, and headlight restoration. These should be optional extras that customers can choose to enhance their main package.

When listing add-ons, explain the benefit briefly. For example, instead of just saying 'Ceramic Coating,' say 'Ceramic Coating for long-lasting shine and protection.' This helps customers understand the value. Ensure that your add-ons do not conflict with the main package services. For instance, do not offer a basic wash add-on to a customer who already bought the comprehensive detail.

  • Ceramic coating for durability.
  • Odor removal for pet or smoke smells.
  • Headlight restoration for better visibility.

Optimize Your Menu for Mobile Viewing

Most of your clients will view your menu on a phone. Therefore, your layout must be simple and easy to read. Use large, clear headings for each package. Avoid long paragraphs of text. Instead, use bullet points to list what is included. This makes it easy for customers to scan the information quickly.

Use high-quality images of your work next to each package description. A photo of a shiny car next to the comprehensive package helps visualize the result. Ensure your text is legible on small screens. Use a font size that is at least sixteen pixels. Keep your contact information and booking button visible at all times. This reduces friction and makes it easier for customers to book their appointment.

  • Use large headings and bullet points.
  • Include high-quality before-and-after photos.
  • Ensure text is legible on small screens.
  • Keep booking buttons visible and accessible.

What to Ask New Dog Walking Clients

TheFixer Designs guide · 2026-11-08

Starting a dog walking business is exciting, but the foundation of a successful client relationship lies in clear communication from day one. Before you ever walk a dog, you need to understand their unique needs, your logistical setup, and the boundaries of your professional relationship. A thorough intake process protects you, the client, and most importantly, the animal in your care. This guide outlines the essential questions to ask new clients to ensure a smooth, safe, and professional start to your partnership.

Essential Pet Details and Health History

Begin by gathering comprehensive information about the dog’s daily life and medical background. You need to know their name, breed, age, and weight to gauge their physical capabilities. Ask about any known medical conditions, such as arthritis, heart issues, or diabetes, which may require medication administration or specific handling during walks.

It is also vital to understand the dog’s personality and triggers. Does the dog pull on the leash? Are they reactive to other dogs, cats, or strangers? Knowing these details allows you to prepare the right equipment and choose safe walking routes. Ask if the dog has any favorite toys or treats that can be used for positive reinforcement or distraction during challenging moments.

  • Full name, breed, age, and weight
  • Current medications and dosing schedule
  • Known allergies or dietary restrictions
  • Triggers (other dogs, loud noises, vehicles)
  • Preferred walking route or areas to avoid

Veterinary and Emergency Contacts

In the event of an injury or sudden illness, you must have immediate access to professional help. Ask for the name, address, and phone number of the dog’s primary veterinarian. Ensure you have the contact information for the nearest emergency animal hospital, as your regular vet may not be available after hours.

You also need a reliable local contact who can grant you access to the home if you are already inside when an emergency occurs. This person should be able to let you in quickly to retrieve the dog or provide necessary supplies. Verify that this contact is available and willing to help in urgent situations.

  • Primary veterinarian’s name and phone number
  • Nearest emergency animal hospital details
  • Local contact’s name, phone, and address
  • Authorization to seek emergency veterinary care
  • Pet insurance policy number and provider info

Access, Keys, and Security Protocols

Clear instructions on how to enter and exit the client’s home are critical for reliability and security. Ask how you will receive keys, gate codes, or alarm passcodes. Determine if you need to disarm a security system and, if so, who will rearm it upon departure. Clarify where spare keys should be kept if the client is not home to hand them over.

Discuss your protocol for locking doors and securing the property after you leave. Some clients may have specific preferences, such as closing windows or turning off lights. Ensure you have a clear plan for returning keys or codes at the end of the service period to maintain the client’s peace of mind.

  • Method of key or code delivery
  • Alarm system disarm and rearm instructions
  • Specific entry and exit points to use
  • Protocol for securing doors and windows
  • Plan for returning keys or access devices

Walking Habits and Daily Routine

Understand the dog’s typical schedule and energy levels. Ask about their usual feeding times, bathroom breaks, and play preferences. Some dogs thrive on long, exploratory walks, while others prefer short, frequent potty breaks. Knowing these preferences helps you tailor the service to the dog’s comfort and the owner’s expectations.

Clarify what happens when you are not walking the dog. Do they need to be crated, left in a specific room, or allowed free roam? Ask about any specific behaviors you should monitor, such as chewing on furniture or excessive barking. This information helps you manage the dog’s behavior effectively during your visit.

  • Preferred walking duration and intensity
  • Feeding schedule and portion sizes
  • Bathroom break frequency
  • Indoor behavior expectations (crate vs. free roam)
  • Specific commands or cues the dog knows

Cancellation Policy and Written Permission

Protect your business by establishing clear terms for scheduling and cancellations. Define how much notice is required for both you and the client to cancel a walk without penalty. Specify how you handle last-minute changes, such as illness or weather emergencies. This clarity prevents misunderstandings and ensures you can plan your route efficiently.

Finally, obtain written permission for you to act on the dog’s behalf in emergencies. This document should authorize you to seek veterinary care if the owner cannot be reached immediately. It also serves as a formal agreement outlining your responsibilities, the client’s duties, and the scope of services provided. Both parties should sign this document before the first walk.

  • Required notice period for cancellations
  • Policy for weather-related or illness cancellations
  • Written authorization for emergency vet care
  • Signed service agreement outlining terms
  • Contact method for urgent updates during walks

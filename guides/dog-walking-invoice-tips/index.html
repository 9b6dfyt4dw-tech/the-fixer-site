Invoicing Tips for Dog Walkers and Pet Sitters

TheFixer Designs guide · 2026-10-25

Dog walking and pet sitting are built on trust and routine. Clients hand you their keys and their pets, so the way you bill them should feel just as dependable. A clear invoice answers every question before it is asked: which visits happened, how many pets, what was extra, and when payment is due. Here is how to set one up so it works week after week.

Choose between per-walk and weekly billing

Per-walk billing lists every visit as its own line with the date, time window and length of the walk. It suits clients with irregular schedules and makes it easy to see exactly what they are paying for.

Weekly or monthly billing groups a regular schedule into one line, such as five weekday walks, and then adds any extras below it. It cuts down on paperwork for steady clients and makes your income easier to plan. Whichever you choose, keep the visit dates visible so the client can check them against their own calendar.

Show multiple pets and add-ons clearly

Many walkers charge an additional amount for a second or third dog from the same household. Put that on its own line instead of folding it into the walk price, so the client understands why the total changed.

  • Extra dog on the same walk
  • Feeding, medication or litter box care during a visit
  • Longer walks or a switch from group to solo walks
  • Holiday, early-morning or late-evening visits
  • Key pickup, lockbox setup or a meet-and-greet visit

Put your cancellation policy on every invoice

Last-minute cancellations are one of the most common sources of friction in pet care. Decide on a simple rule, such as how much notice you need before a visit is cancelled without charge, and print it at the bottom of each invoice.

When a late cancellation does happen, list it as a separate line with the date and a short note. Seeing the rule and the charge side by side keeps the conversation calm and factual.

Handle overnight stays and pet sitting

Overnight stays and house sitting are usually billed per night, often with a different amount for weekends or holidays. List each night with its date and keep extra services, like plant watering or bringing in the mail, on separate lines.

For longer bookings, ask for a deposit when the dates are confirmed and show it on the final invoice as already paid. The client sees the full picture and only the balance remains.

Make payment terms simple

Regular clients are easiest to manage when payment is due on a predictable day, such as every Friday for that week of walks or on the first of the month for monthly plans. Say it plainly on the invoice and list the payment methods you accept.

  • A clear due date, not just a number of days
  • Accepted payment methods and the reference to include
  • What happens with overdue balances, stated politely
  • Your contact details for schedule changes

Keep records for your own peace of mind

Number every invoice and keep a copy, even for small amounts. A tidy record helps at tax time, makes it easy to answer questions about past visits, and shows clients you run a real business. Check local rules on what records you need to keep.

What Every Handyman Invoice Should Include

TheFixer Designs guide · 2026-10-25

Handyman work is a mix of small jobs, materials and time, often all on the same visit. That variety is great for business but can make invoices confusing. A well organised handyman invoice turns a busy day of fixes into a clear list the client understands at a glance, which means fewer questions and faster payment.

Start with the essentials

Every invoice should identify you, the client and the job before it lists any work. These details make the invoice easy to file and easy to find later.

  • Your business name, phone and email
  • The client name and the job address
  • An invoice number and the invoice date
  • The date or dates the work was done
  • A due date for payment

Itemise each task separately

Clients remember jobs by task, not by hours. Instead of one line that says general repairs, list each item: fix the sticking bathroom door, mount the TV in the living room, replace two cabinet hinges. Short, specific lines show the value of the visit and make the total feel fair.

If you price some tasks as a flat fee and others by the hour, label each line so the client can see which is which.

List materials on their own lines

Materials and parts belong in a separate section from labor. Note what was bought, the quantity and the amount charged. If you add a handling or pickup charge for sourcing materials, show it openly rather than hiding it inside the price of the parts.

Keep your receipts with your copy of the invoice. If a client asks about a part, you can answer in seconds.

Be clear about time-based work

For hourly work, show the start and finish time or the total hours, and say whether you bill in full hours or smaller blocks. If you have a minimum charge for a visit or a trip charge for travel, put it on its own line so it never comes as a surprise.

Add notes that protect you and the client

A short notes area at the bottom of the invoice is a good place for anything the client should know after you leave.

  • Any issue you spotted but did not fix, with a recommendation
  • Work the client declined, so there is a record of it
  • Any guarantee you offer on your workmanship
  • Reminders such as letting caulk or paint cure before use

Make it easy to pay

End the invoice with the total, any deposit already received and the balance due, followed by your accepted payment methods. Sending the invoice the same day the work is finished, while the results are fresh, usually leads to quicker payment. Check local rules for anything your invoices must include, such as a license or registration number.

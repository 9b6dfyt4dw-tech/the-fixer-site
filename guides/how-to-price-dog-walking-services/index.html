How to Price Dog Walking Services: Per Walk, Packages and Extras

TheFixer Designs guide · 2026-10-31

Dog walking looks simple to price: pick a number per walk and go. In practice the details decide whether the business works. A half-hour solo walk across town is a very different job from a group walk two streets away, and a client with three energetic dogs is not the same as one with a sleepy senior. This guide covers how to structure dog walking prices so they are fair to clients, cover your real costs and stay simple enough to explain in one message.

On this page

  2. Price by walk length and walk type
  3. Multiple dogs, puppies and special care
  4. Packages, holidays and cancellations

Start from your real cost per hour

Before setting any walk price, work out what an hour of your time costs. Include travel between homes, insurance, any licences or checks you need, leads and supplies, your phone and booking tools, and the unpaid time spent on messages and scheduling.

Add the income you want and divide by the hours you can realistically walk in a week. The result is your floor. A walk price should cover the walk itself plus the travel and admin around it, not just the minutes on the lead.

Price by walk length and walk type

Most dog walkers offer a small menu rather than one price. Keep it short so clients can choose quickly.

  • Walk length: a shorter and a longer option, each with its own price.
  • Solo walks: one dog or one household, with full attention, priced higher.
  • Group walks: several compatible dogs together, priced lower per dog.
  • Drop-in visits: a quick toilet break, feed or check-in, priced separately from walks.

Multiple dogs, puppies and special care

A second or third dog from the same home adds work but usually not a full extra walk. Many walkers add a set amount for each additional dog. Be clear about how many dogs you can safely walk at once.

Puppies, reactive dogs, very large breeds and dogs needing medication take more time and attention. Price these as an add-on or a separate service so your standard price stays clean.

Packages, holidays and cancellations

Weekly or monthly packages give clients a simple routine and give you predictable income. Offer a package price for a fixed number of walks per week, billed in advance.

Decide your rules for public holidays, early mornings and late evenings, and say them in writing. Do the same for cancellations: how much notice you need, and what happens to a walk cancelled at the last minute.

Put it in writing and invoice clearly

Send new clients a short price list and terms before the first walk: walk types, prices, extras, package rules, payment timing and cancellation policy. It prevents most disagreements before they start.

When you invoice, list each walk or the package period, any extras, and the dates covered. Clients who can see exactly what they are paying for pay faster and stay longer.

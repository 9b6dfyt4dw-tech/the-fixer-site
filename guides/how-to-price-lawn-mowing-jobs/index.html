How to Price Lawn Mowing Jobs: Per Visit, Per Season or By Size

TheFixer Designs guide · 2026-10-31

Lawn mowing looks simple to price until you realise no two properties take the same time. A flat open lawn and a sloped yard full of flower beds can look similar on a map but take very different effort. Price them the same and you either lose jobs or lose money. This guide explains the common ways to price mowing, how to size up a property quickly, and how to turn it all into a quote that clients understand and accept.

Know your cost per hour first

Before choosing a pricing method, work out what an hour of your time really costs. Include fuel, equipment wear and replacement, insurance, your vehicle, phone and software, and the time you spend travelling and quoting rather than mowing.

Add the income you need and divide by the hours you can realistically bill in a season. That number is your floor. Every pricing method below should land at or above it once you estimate how long a job takes.

Three common pricing methods

Most lawn care businesses use one of these, or a mix.

  • Per visit: one price for each mow, based on the time the property takes. Simple for clients and easy to adjust.
  • By lawn size: a price band for small, medium and large lawns, with adjustments for difficulty. Quick to quote once you know your bands.
  • Seasonal contract: an agreed number of visits across the season, often billed monthly. Gives you predictable income and gives the client a steady service.

Size up the property

Time is what you are really selling, so estimate it carefully on the first visit. Walk the property and note what will slow you down.

After a few visits, compare the time you estimated with the time the job actually took and adjust. Your first quote for a property is a best guess, and it is fine to review it at the start of the next season.

  • Total mowable area and whether a ride-on mower fits.
  • Slopes, uneven ground and tight gates.
  • Obstacles such as beds, trees, play equipment and furniture.
  • Edging and trimming along paths, fences and borders.
  • Whether clippings are bagged and removed, and where they go.

Travel, disposal and add-ons

Travel time between jobs is easy to forget. Grouping clients by area keeps it low, and a minimum visit price makes sure a small lawn far from your route is still worth the trip.

Green waste disposal can carry a real cost. Either include it in your price or list it as its own line. Add-ons such as hedge trimming, leaf clean-ups, aeration or fertilising should be priced separately so the mowing price stays clear.

Write a clear quote

Your quote should describe exactly what a standard visit includes, how often you will visit, the price per visit or per month, any add-ons, and the payment terms. Mention what happens if a visit is skipped because of weather, and how price changes are handled from one season to the next.

When you invoice, use the same descriptions as the quote. For seasonal clients, list each visit or the billing period so the client can see what they are paying for.

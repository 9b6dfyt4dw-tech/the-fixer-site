How to Price Personal Training Sessions and Packages

TheFixer Designs guide · 2026-10-31

Personal trainers often start by charging per session and quickly run into problems: clients who cancel last minute, packs that never expire, and online check-ins that take hours but earn nothing. A clear pricing structure fixes most of this. This guide walks through how to price single sessions, packages, small group and online coaching, and how to write the terms that protect your time.

On this page

  1. Know your cost per session
  2. Single sessions versus packs
  3. Small group and online coaching
  4. Travel, expiry and cancellations
  5. Invoice packages clearly

Know your cost per session

Start with what a session really costs you. Include gym or studio fees, equipment, insurance, certifications and continuing education, travel, software, and the time you spend programming, messaging clients and marketing.

Add your target income and divide by the sessions you can realistically deliver each week. That number is the floor for a single session. Packages and group options can sit around it, but none of them should fall below it once you count the extra admin they bring.

Single sessions versus packs

A single session price is useful for trial sessions and occasional clients. Packs of sessions reward commitment and give you predictable income. A small saving per session in a larger pack is common, as long as the pack is paid in advance.

Keep the number of options small. Two or three pack sizes are easier to explain than a long list, and clients decide faster.

  • Single session: for trials and occasional bookings.
  • Small pack: a few sessions, paid upfront.
  • Large pack or monthly plan: regular sessions with the best per-session value.

Small group and online coaching

Small group training lets you charge each person less while earning more per hour. Set a maximum group size so quality stays high, and price per person per session or as a monthly membership.

Online coaching is mostly programming, check-ins and messages. Price it as a monthly plan that states exactly what is included, such as program updates and how often you reply, so it does not quietly turn into unlimited unpaid work.

Travel, expiry and cancellations

If you train clients at home or in parks, add a travel charge or a minimum session length so travel time is covered. Group nearby clients on the same days where you can.

Give every pack an expiry date so sessions do not hang over for months. Set a cancellation window and decide what happens to a late cancellation, usually that the session counts as used. Put these rules in writing before the first session.

Invoice packages clearly

Invoice packs when they are bought, showing the pack size, price, expiry date and payment terms. Keep a simple record of sessions used so you and the client can always see how many remain.

For monthly plans, invoice at the start of each month and list what the plan includes. Clear invoices make renewals feel routine rather than awkward.

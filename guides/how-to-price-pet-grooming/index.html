How to Price Pet Grooming: By Size, Coat and Service

TheFixer Designs guide · 2026-10-31

Pet grooming prices go wrong when every dog is charged the same. A small short-coated dog and a large double-coated one with tangles can take very different amounts of time, product and patience. A clear price structure lets you quote quickly, charge fairly for the hard jobs and explain your prices without awkward conversations at pick-up. This guide covers how to build that structure step by step.

Start from your cost per hour

Every grooming price should rest on what an hour of your time really costs. Count rent or van costs, water and power, shampoos and tools, blade sharpening, insurance, booking software, and the time spent cleaning and handling messages.

Add your target income and divide by the grooming hours you can realistically fill each week. That number is your floor, and each service price should cover the time it usually takes at that rate.

Build a base price by size and coat

Most groomers use a simple grid: size bands across one side and coat type or service level across the other. Keep the bands few and clear so owners can find their pet quickly.

  • Size: small, medium, large and extra large, defined by weight or breed examples.
  • Coat: short and smooth, double coat, long or curly coats that need scissoring.
  • Service: bath and tidy, full groom, or a specific style.

Matting, behaviour and special handling

Matted coats, anxious or reactive pets and senior animals take extra time and care. Price these as clearly described surcharges rather than quietly absorbing the time, and tell owners in advance when a surcharge may apply.

Explain why: dematting takes longer and can be uncomfortable for the pet, so a shave-down may be the kinder option. Owners accept surcharges more easily when they understand the reason and hear about it before the appointment.

Add-ons and mobile grooming

Keep popular extras as separate line items so base prices stay simple: nail trims, teeth brushing, de-shedding treatments, ear cleaning and special shampoos. Owners like being able to choose.

Mobile groomers should also account for travel. A call-out charge or a minimum appointment value keeps short trips worthwhile, and grouping appointments by area keeps driving time down.

Terms and a clear invoice

Write down your no-show and late cancellation rules, how far ahead clients should book, and what happens if a pet arrives in worse condition than expected. Share these when a client books for the first time.

On the invoice, list the base service by size, each add-on and any surcharge with a short reason. A clear breakdown turns a potentially awkward conversation at pick-up into a simple, transparent bill.

How to Price Photography Sessions and Packages

TheFixer Designs guide · 2026-10-31

Many new photographers price by the hour they spend behind the camera and wonder why the business struggles. The session is only part of the job. Planning, travel, culling, editing, delivering the gallery and answering messages often take longer than the shoot itself. This guide explains how to count all of that time, structure session fees and packages, and write quotes that clients understand.

Count the whole job, not just the shoot

Start by listing every step of a typical session and how long it takes: the enquiry and planning, travel, the shoot, culling, editing, exporting and delivery, and follow-up messages. Many photographers find the shoot is only a small part of the total.

Then add your costs: camera and lens replacement over time, software subscriptions, insurance, gallery hosting, backups and marketing. Divide your yearly costs and target income by the number of sessions you can realistically do. That gives you the minimum a session must earn.

Session fees and packages

One common approach is a session fee that covers your time, with images and products sold separately. Another is packages that bundle the session with a set number of edited images. Both work, as long as each option covers the full time involved.

Keep packages to a small number of clear options so clients can compare them at a glance.

  • A short session with a small set of edited images.
  • A standard session with a larger gallery.
  • An extended or premium option with extra locations, outfits or prints.

Digital files, prints and usage

Decide how clients receive their images: a fixed number included, extra images sold individually, or the full gallery as an upgrade. Prints and albums can be priced as products with their own margin on top of the print cost.

For business and commercial work, how the images will be used matters. Personal use, social media and advertising are different uses, and commercial usage is usually priced higher. State the usage included in every quote.

Travel, deposits and rescheduling

Include travel within a set distance and charge for travel beyond it. For outdoor sessions, explain what happens if the weather is bad, such as a free reschedule within a time window.

Take a deposit to secure the date, especially for weddings and events, and show it clearly on the quote and the final invoice. Write down your cancellation terms so clients know what happens if plans change.

Write a clear quote and invoice

A good photography quote lists the session or package, what is included, the number of edited images, delivery time, usage rights, travel, the deposit and payment terms. It removes guesswork for both sides.

When you invoice, use the same line items as the quote, add any extras the client chose, subtract the deposit and show the balance due. Consistent paperwork makes your business look as professional as your photos.

How to Write a Plumbing Invoice (What to Include)

TheFixer Designs guide · 2026-10-19

Running a small plumbing business means you spend most of your day fixing leaks and unclogging drains, but the job isn't done until the paperwork is right. A clear, professional invoice does more than just ask for money; it builds trust, prevents payment delays, and protects your business from disputes. When a customer receives a well-organized bill, they feel confident in the work you did and understand exactly what they are paying for. This guide breaks down the essential elements of a plumbing invoice to help you get paid faster and keep your clients happy.

  2. Breaking Down Labor and Call-Out Fees
  3. Listing Parts and Materials Clearly

Essential Header Information

Before listing any costs, ensure your contact details and the customer's information are prominent at the top. This establishes professionalism immediately and ensures there is no confusion about who sent the bill or who it is for. Include your business name, logo, address, phone number, and email, along with the client's name, property address, and the date of service.

You should also include a unique invoice number and the specific date the work was performed. This helps both you and the customer track the transaction easily if questions arise later. A clear header sets the stage for a transparent relationship from the very first line of the document.

  • Your business name, logo, and full contact details
  • Customer's name and service address
  • Invoice number and date of issue
  • Date(s) the work was completed

Breaking Down Labor and Call-Out Fees

Labor charges are often the most confusing part for customers, so be specific about what you did. Clearly state whether you charge by the hour or a flat fee for the job, and show the rate you used. If you charge a call-out fee for traveling to the site, list this as a separate line item with a brief description, such as 'Travel and dispatch fee'.

If the job took longer than expected due to unforeseen complications, note that in the description. This transparency shows the customer that you are not inflating costs arbitrarily but are billing for the actual time spent solving their problem. Clear labor descriptions prevent arguments about why a bill is higher than they anticipated.

  • Hourly rate or flat fee for the labor
  • Total hours worked or specific task duration
  • Call-out or travel fee description
  • Notes on any unexpected delays or complications

Listing Parts and Materials Clearly

Customers want to know exactly what physical items they are buying. List every part used, from the smallest washer to the largest pipe section, with a brief description of each item. You can include the quantity and unit price for transparency, or simply list the total cost for materials if you prefer a simpler look.

It is helpful to distinguish between parts you supplied and those the customer provided themselves. If you used high-quality replacements that extend the life of their plumbing, mention this briefly in the notes. This reinforces the value of your work and justifies the material costs without needing to cite specific brand names or prices.

  • Itemized list of all parts and materials used
  • Quantity and description for each item
  • Total cost for materials
  • Note if any parts were customer-supplied

Warranty Notes and Payment Terms

Including a warranty note is a powerful way to reassure your customers that you stand behind your work. Clearly state the duration of your labor warranty and what it covers, such as '90-day guarantee on all labor'. This reduces anxiety for the client and encourages them to pay promptly because they know they are protected.

Payment terms must be explicit to avoid confusion about when money is due. State clearly if payment is required upon completion, or if there is a net-15 or net-30 window. List your accepted payment methods, such as cash, check, credit card, or bank transfer, and provide instructions on how to make the payment.

  • Duration of labor warranty (e.g., '90-day guarantee')
  • Scope of what is covered under warranty
  • Due date for payment
  • Accepted payment methods and instructions

Invoice Payment Terms Explained for Small Businesses

TheFixer Designs guide · 2026-10-25

Payment terms tell clients when and how to pay you. They seem like small print, but clear terms are one of the easiest ways to get paid on time. Vague wording such as "payment due soon" invites delays, while a specific date and a simple set of rules makes paying you the obvious next step. Here are the common payment terms small businesses use and how to show them clearly.

Common payment terms

Choose terms that fit your type of work and your clients. Whatever you choose, use the same wording every time so regular clients learn your rhythm.

  • Due on receipt: payment is expected as soon as the invoice arrives, common for small jobs and one-off services
  • Net 7, Net 14 or Net 30: payment is due a set number of days after the invoice date
  • Due before delivery: the balance is paid before files, products or keys are handed over
  • Deposit plus balance: part is paid to book the work and the rest on completion
  • Recurring billing: regular clients pay on the same date each week or month

Write a real date, not just a term

Terms like Net 14 are clear to you but easy for a busy client to misread. Add the actual due date on the invoice as well, for example "Due: 14 November". A specific date removes guesswork and gives both of you a clear point to follow up from.

Deposits and staged payments

For larger jobs, a deposit protects your time and covers materials you buy upfront. Show the deposit on the estimate, confirm it when it is paid and subtract it on the final invoice. For long projects, staged payments at clear milestones keep cash flowing and make large totals easier for clients to manage.

Say plainly whether a deposit is refundable and under what conditions. Clear deposit rules prevent most cancellation arguments.

Make paying easy

The easier it is to pay, the faster money arrives. List every payment method you accept, the exact details a client needs and the reference they should include so you can match the payment to the invoice. If you accept online payments, a direct link on the invoice removes most of the friction.

Late payments, handled politely

Decide in advance what you will do if a payment is late, and put a short, polite line about it on your invoices. Many businesses send a friendly reminder a day or two after the due date and a firmer one a week later. If you plan to charge late fees, check local rules first and state the policy clearly on every invoice so it never comes as a surprise.

Clear payment terms are a quiet form of good customer service. Pick terms that suit your work, write the actual due date, explain deposits, make paying simple and decide your late-payment approach in advance. Most clients want to pay on time, and clear terms help them do exactly that.

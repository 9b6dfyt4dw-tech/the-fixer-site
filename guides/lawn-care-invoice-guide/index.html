How to Invoice Lawn Care and Landscaping Jobs

TheFixer Designs guide · October 25, 2026 · 3 min read

Lawn care and landscaping invoices have to cover very different kinds of work: weekly mowing that repeats all season, one-off clean-ups, and projects that need materials like mulch, plants or stone. Putting all of that on one clear invoice keeps clients informed and keeps your cash flow steady through the busy months.

On this page

  1. Separate recurring and one-off services
  2. Bill materials and disposal openly
  3. Plan for the seasons
  4. Choose a billing rhythm
  5. Note weather and skipped visits
  6. Finish with clear payment details

Separate recurring and one-off services

Recurring work such as mowing, edging and blowing is easiest to bill as a single line per visit or per period, with the visit dates listed. One-off jobs like a spring clean-up, hedge trimming or gutter clearing should sit on their own lines below it.

This split helps the client see their normal monthly cost at a glance and understand why a particular invoice is higher than usual.

Bill materials and disposal openly

Landscaping often involves products you buy and haul. List each material with its quantity and unit, such as bags, yards or flats, and show any delivery or disposal charge as a separate line.

  • Mulch, soil, seed or sod with quantities
  • Plants and shrubs by type and count
  • Green waste removal or dump fees
  • Equipment rental for a specific job

Plan for the seasons

Your services change through the year, so your invoices should too. Spring might bring aeration and clean-ups, summer is mostly mowing and watering checks, autumn brings leaf removal, and some businesses add snow clearing in winter.

Use consistent names for each seasonal service so clients recognise them year after year, and consider a short note at the end of the season about what you recommend next.

Choose a billing rhythm

Per-visit billing works well for occasional clients. Monthly billing suits regular customers and means fewer invoices to send. Some businesses offer a seasonal agreement billed in equal monthly installments; if you do, show which month the invoice covers and how many installments remain.

Note weather and skipped visits

Rain, drought and heat waves all change the schedule. When a visit is skipped or moved, note it on the invoice so the client is not charged for a cut that did not happen, or knows when the make-up visit took place. That small line builds a lot of trust.

  • Visits skipped because of rain or drought, with the date
  • Make-up visits and the day they happened
  • Extra cuts during fast growth, agreed with the client
  • Watering or irrigation checks added during a heat wave

Finish with clear payment details

Close with the total, any deposit or prepayment, the balance due and a specific due date. List the payment methods you accept and your contact details for schedule changes. Check local rules for anything your invoices must show, such as business registration details.

How to Build a Price List for a Mobile Detailing Business

TheFixer Designs guide · 2026-10-24

Creating a clear price list is one of the most powerful tools you can use to grow your mobile detailing business. A well-organized menu helps customers feel confident in their choices and saves you time explaining services on the phone or in person. Instead of guessing what to charge, this guide will help you structure your packages and add-ons so they look professional and make sense to anyone looking to get their vehicle clean.

Start with Clear Service Tiers

Group your services into distinct tiers, such as a Basic Wash, a Standard Detail, and a Premium Restoration. This allows customers to quickly identify the level of care they need without feeling overwhelmed by too many options. Keep the names simple and descriptive so there is no confusion about what is included in each package.

Ensure that each tier offers a logical progression in value. A customer should easily see why the higher-tier package costs more, perhaps due to longer labor time, specialized products, or extra steps like clay bar treatment. This structure helps guide clients toward the service that best fits their budget and vehicle condition.

  • Define exactly what is included in each tier (e.g., exterior wash, interior vacuum, window cleaning).
  • Use consistent naming conventions across all your marketing materials.
  • Ensure the lowest tier covers the absolute essentials for a clean car.

List Add-Ons as Standalone Options

Not every customer needs a full interior deep clean or paint correction, so list these as optional add-ons that can be attached to any base package. This flexibility allows you to tailor the quote to the specific condition of the car and the owner's budget. It also makes it easier for customers to upgrade their service later if they decide they want more.

Be specific about what each add-on entails so there are no surprises on the day of the job. For example, clearly state that a 'Leather Conditioning' add-on includes cleaning, conditioning, and UV protection, rather than just listing the words 'leather care.'

Consider grouping related add-ons together, such as 'Interior Protection' or 'Exterior Enhancement,' to make the list easier to scan.

  • List common extras like engine bay cleaning, headlight restoration, and odor removal.
  • Specify if an add-on requires a minimum vehicle size (e.g., SUV vs. sedan).
  • Indicate which add-ons are best paired with specific base packages.

Define Vehicle Size Categories

Vehicle size is the single biggest factor in how long a job takes, so your price list must account for this clearly. Create simple categories like Compact/Sedan, SUV/Truck, and Large/Van to avoid confusion when quoting different types of vehicles. This prevents disputes later about why a larger vehicle costs more than a smaller one.

You can either list separate base prices for each size category or include a clear surcharge amount for moving up a size class. The key is consistency; always apply the same rules for every customer so your business feels fair and organized.

If you have vehicles that fall outside standard categories, such as luxury boats or oversized RVs, note that these require a custom quote to ensure you are compensated fairly for the extra time and effort.

  • Set clear size limits for each category (e.g., 'Sedan up to 15 feet').
  • State the price difference or surcharge for moving to the next size tier.
  • Include a note about how you measure vehicle size if it is not obvious.

Clarify Travel and Scheduling Policies

Since you are running a mobile business, travel time and location are critical components of your pricing. Clearly state your service radius where the base price applies, and define what happens when a customer is located further away. This transparency builds trust and ensures you do not lose money on long drives.

Include a section on scheduling to manage expectations about availability. Mention if there are rush fees for same-day appointments or discounts for booking multiple vehicles at once. Being upfront about these policies helps customers plan ahead and reduces last-minute stress for both parties.

Remember to check local rules regarding any specific permits or fees required for working in certain residential areas or commercial parking lots.

  • Define your free travel radius clearly.
  • List the fee structure for distances beyond your standard zone.
  • Outline your cancellation policy and any rush service fees.

A well-structured price list does more than just tell customers how much you charge; it tells them exactly what they are buying and why it is worth the investment. Take the time to review your list regularly to ensure it reflects your current costs and service quality, and remember that clarity is the key to converting inquiries into bookings.

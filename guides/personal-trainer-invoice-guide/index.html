Invoicing for Personal Trainers: Sessions and Packages

TheFixer Designs guide · 2026-10-25

Personal training is built on regular sessions and long-term progress, and your billing should feel just as steady. Some clients pay per session, others buy a pack or a monthly plan, and more trainers now add online check-ins between workouts. A clear invoice keeps all of that organised so clients always know where they stand.

Single sessions vs. session packs

For pay-as-you-go clients, list each session with its date, length and type, such as one-to-one, partner or small group training.

For session packs, invoice the pack as one line when it is bought, then show how many sessions remain on later invoices or receipts. Clients love seeing their progress through a pack, and it reminds them when it is time to renew.

Monthly plans and online coaching

Monthly coaching plans are easiest to bill on the same date each month. Describe what the plan includes so the client sees the full value, not just a number.

  • Number of in-person sessions per month
  • Training program updates
  • Online check-ins or video calls
  • Messaging support between sessions
  • Nutrition guidance, if you offer it within your qualifications

Put your cancellation policy in writing

Late cancellations and no-shows are a common headache for trainers. Decide how much notice you need, and whether a missed session is charged or deducted from a pack, then print that rule on every invoice. When a late cancellation happens, list it on its own line with the date.

Extras and add-ons

Keep anything outside the normal plan on separate lines so the main package stays easy to understand. Examples include extra sessions, fitness assessments, travel to a client's home or outdoor location, and equipment the client keeps.

Partner and small group training

When two or more people train together, decide whether you bill one person for the whole session or split it between them, and say so on the invoice. Splitting is fair but means more invoices; billing one lead client is simpler when the group is a couple or family.

For small group classes, list the class name, date and number of spots booked. If clients buy a class pass, show the remaining classes in the same way as a session pack so everyone knows when to renew.

Keep tidy records

Number every invoice in order, note the period it covers and keep a copy of each one. Good records make renewals, refunds and questions about past sessions quick to answer, and they make tax time far less stressful.

Clear payment terms

Many trainers ask for packs and monthly plans to be paid in advance, before the first session of the period. Show the due date, accepted payment methods and what happens to unpaid sessions, stated politely. Number every invoice and keep copies for your records, and check local rules on what your invoices need to include.

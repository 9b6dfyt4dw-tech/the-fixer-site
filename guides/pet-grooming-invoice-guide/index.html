How to Invoice Pet Grooming Appointments

TheFixer Designs guide · 2026-10-25

Grooming appointments vary with every pet: size, coat, temperament and the extras an owner asks for on the day. That makes a clear invoice especially important. When owners can see exactly what was done for their pet, they understand the total, trust your pricing and book again.

On this page

  1. Identify the pet and the appointment
  2. Price services by size and coat
  3. List add-ons separately
  4. Handle multiple pets and regular clients
  5. Use the invoice for care notes
  6. Changing prices without surprises
  7. Policies and payment

Identify the pet and the appointment

Start with the details that make the invoice easy to find later, especially for households with more than one pet.

  • Owner name and contact details
  • Pet name, breed and size category
  • Appointment date and invoice number
  • Any notes on coat condition or behaviour that affected the groom

Price services by size and coat

Most groomers price a full groom or bath by size and coat type, because a large double-coated dog takes much longer than a small short-haired one. Name the size category on the invoice so owners see why the price differs between their pets.

If a coat is matted or needs extra de-shedding time, list that as its own line with a short explanation, rather than quietly raising the main price.

List add-ons separately

Add-ons are where invoices often get confusing. Give each one its own line so the owner can see exactly what was done.

  • Nail trim or grinding
  • Ear cleaning
  • Teeth brushing
  • Paw pad and sanitary trim
  • Flea treatment or medicated shampoo
  • Bows, bandanas or finishing spray

Handle multiple pets and regular clients

For households with several pets, group each pet's services under its name, then show one combined total. Regular clients on a set schedule, such as every few weeks, appreciate the next appointment date printed at the bottom, which also helps fill your calendar.

Use the invoice for care notes

A short note at the bottom of the invoice is a natural place to share what you noticed during the groom: a small skin irritation, a sore spot the owner may want a vet to check, or a coat that would benefit from more frequent brushing at home. Keep the note factual and friendly, and avoid diagnosing anything yourself.

Owners value this kind of attention, and a written note protects you too if a question comes up later about the condition of the pet when it arrived.

Changing prices without surprises

If you raise prices or change how you charge for a size category, add a short line to invoices a few weeks beforehand so regular clients hear about it from you first. Clear notice keeps trust high and avoids awkward conversations at pickup.

Policies and payment

Print your key policies where owners will see them: late arrival, missed appointments and how you handle pets that need to be calmed or finished early for their comfort. End with the total, payment methods and due date, which for most grooming appointments is at pickup. Check local rules on what your invoices must include.

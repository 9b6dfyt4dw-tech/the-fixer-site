Quote Follow-Up Planner: When to Chase and What to Say

TheFixer Designs guide · 2026-11-10

Most quotes that go quiet are not a no. The client got busy, or is waiting for you to make the next move. This free planner gives you three follow-up dates from the day you sent the quote, with a short message for each one that you can copy, adjust and send.

On this page

  1. Why three messages and then stop
  2. Make each message easy to answer
  3. Put an expiry date on the quote
The quote

First check-in: -

Two days after sending. Just make sure it arrived.

Second follow-up: -

One week after sending. Offer to answer questions or adjust the quote.

Last message: -

Two weeks after sending. Close the loop politely and move on.

Dates that land on a weekend move to the Monday after. Nothing you type leaves this page.

Why three messages and then stop

One check-in shows you are organised. A second, a week later, catches clients who meant to reply. A third closes the loop so the quote is not left hanging and your calendar is free for other work. More than three starts to feel like pressure, and it rarely changes the answer.

Make each message easy to answer

Keep it short, name the job, and give the client one simple next step: ask a question, request a change or say yes. Avoid discounts in a follow-up unless you planned one. A lower price offered out of silence teaches clients to wait.

Put an expiry date on the quote

A line such as "This quote is valid for 30 days" gives you a natural reason to follow up and protects you if material prices move.

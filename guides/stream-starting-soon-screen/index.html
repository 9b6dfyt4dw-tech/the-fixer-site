What to Put on a Starting Soon Screen

TheFixer Designs guide · 2026-10-23

The starting-soon screen is the first thing a viewer sees, and most of them decide in a few seconds whether to wait. It has one job: tell people the stream is real, it is about to start, and what they will get if they stay. This guide covers the three screens every stream needs, what each one should say, and the exact sizes OBS and Streamlabs expect.

The three screens

ScreenWhenWhat it must say
Starting soon5 to 10 minutes before you go liveYour name, what today's stream is, and a start time or countdown
Be right backShort breaksThat you will return, roughly when, and a reason to stay (chat, music, a clip)
Stream endingLast minute of the streamThanks, next stream date, and where to follow

Starting-soon screen: the five lines

  1. Who you are. Your channel name, large. Viewers who arrive from a raid or a share may not know it.
  2. What is happening today. One line: the game, the topic, the project. "Building the shed, part 3" beats "Chill stream".
  3. When it starts. A clock time in your viewer's time zone, or a countdown. A countdown is the single biggest reason people wait.
  4. What to do while they wait. Say hello in chat, follow, drop a song request. Give them one action.
  5. One social handle. Not six. The one you actually answer.

Keep the text in the middle 80% of the screen. Twitch and YouTube overlays, chat boxes and the mobile layout crop the edges.

Be-right-back screen

Short and honest. "Back in 5" with a timer is enough. Add your music or a highlight clip so the audio does not go dead; silence makes people leave faster than any wait does. If you use a timer, reset it before each break so it never counts up past zero on stream.

Stream-ending screen

Thank them, then tell them when you are back. "Next stream: Thursday 7 pm" is the line that brings people back. Leave it up for at least 60 seconds so the clip and VOD end cleanly, and raid out from this screen if you raid.

Sizes and formats

Add the image as an Image Source in its own scene, then switch scenes to show it. For a countdown, add a Text source on top and point it at a text file your timer updates, or use the countdown plugin your streaming software ships with.

Common mistakes

  • No start time. "Starting soon" with no number is a shrug. Give a time or a countdown.
  • Tiny text. Most viewers are on a phone. Test the screen at phone size; the headline should still be readable.
  • A different look each time. The three screens should share colours, fonts and layout so the channel feels like one place.
  • Dead audio. Put music on every waiting screen.
